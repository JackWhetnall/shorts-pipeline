# 137 — A teaser opens on its film's most arresting picture

**Status:** active. Adds to 106 and 111.

## What the owner said (27 Oct)

> we should be able to find the images that we already have that most fit
> this category, and use them to our advantage ... rather than opening
> with a generic picture of the steam pumps (for example, that is one
> opening shot of a teaser), we open with something that is more likely to
> make people stay around.

This came from a widely known pattern, not from our own handful of
videos: people stop for a person, and an attractive, expressive one, far
more than for an object or a landscape. Nothing explicit or suggestive is
drawn. The film's existing pictures are chosen with this in mind.

## Decision

- **The teaser writer** opens each teaser on the film's picture most
  likely to stop a scroller, from the part of the film that teaser takes:
  - a person large in the frame, rather than an object, a machine, a map
    or a landscape;
  - an attractive, expressive person mid-action, rather than a small or
    distant figure.

  The first line must still be true over it. A picture from an unrelated
  part of the film is never chosen only for its looks.
- **The teaser editor** looks at the film's pictures (`newsroom frames`)
  and swaps any teaser's first picture for a better one with
  `edit-teaser ... --picture N` (Checkpoint 3, item 10). Only the film's own
  drawn pictures can be chosen, and none twice in one teaser.

Topics aren't narrowed: this decides only which existing picture comes
first.

**Teasers already made** keep their openings. Changing one means voicing it
again, about 200 credits, which can be done after the 7 Nov reset if
wanted.
