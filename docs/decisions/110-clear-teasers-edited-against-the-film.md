# 110 — Clear teasers, edited against the film

**Status:** active. It replaces the tone and ending rules of decisions
106 and 109; their structure (hook, premise, payoff, invitation) stands.

## What happened

Three rounds of "more zing", "punchier" and "the way a friend would say
it" made the teasers worse. The owner pointed out:
- light getting "shredded";
- Everest that "still wouldn't touch bottom";
- "That part's wild. Go watch the full video on our channel."

The instructions had piled up into a request for attitude, and the
writer supplied slang and strained images instead of clear sentences.
The fact check saw only the teasers' own lines, so a comparison the
writer invented or garbled went through. One example: twelve kilometres
became "about the length of a half marathon", which is twenty-one.

Checking that example showed that some of the oddness comes from the
films themselves. The film's own narration says twelve kilometres is
"about the length of a lunchtime jog"; it also has "one grain of sugar
in a swimming pool", and Everest "flipped upside down and dropped
straight into the sea".

## Decision

- **One clean brief instead of the accumulated rules.**
  - The teaser sounds like the film's narrator: clear, warm, curious and
    plain. Every sentence must make literal sense to a first-time viewer.
    "Clarity beats cleverness."
  - No slang, no hype words, and no metaphor the film doesn't use.
  - Figures and comparisons only as the narration states them, and no
    comparisons of the writer's own.
  - The last line is one plain invitation, in the shape of the owner's
    examples: "Check out our channel for the full explanation." "Find out
    in the full video on our channel." Or questions, then "This and more,
    explained on our channel."
  - The writer thinks at high effort.
- **An editor reads the teasers against the film's narration**
  (`teasers.checked(scripts, script)`). It replaces the fact check that
  saw only the teasers' own lines. It fixes any line that:
  - adds or changes a fact or comparison;
  - is unclear or clumsy aloud;
  - uses slang or hype.

  A comparison the film doesn't make is cut, never swapped for another.
- **The ocean film's teaser #2 was remade under these rules.** It's now
  "The Ocean Map Hiding in Plain Sight". Teaser #1 was already approved
  and was kept.

## Open

The films' own narration has a few strained comparisons (above), which
the film's fact check doesn't look for. The same clarity check could run
in the film's editor pass.
