# 093 — Repeated points rewritten, brighter days, real chapter titles

**Status:** active. Builds on 091 and 092.

## What happened

On 13 October the owner found the third drill film "pretty good":

> "the script is a little weak, repeats itself a couple of times ...
> the setting at night is a little dark but fine for one video. I assume
> other videos would be brighter. Please make another topic to prove it
> all works"

## Decisions

**Points made twice are rewritten** (`editor.restatements`, about 3
cents).
- **The gap.** The editor's checks catch a phrase said word for word,
  and a word worn out. They don't catch the same point made again in
  new words. The drill film said "up gets easier, down gets harder"
  about five times.
- **What it does.** One reader pass finds lines that only restate an
  earlier point and rewrites each to move the film on. Up to 10 lines
  are rewritten, never the last line, and one callback to the central
  question in a chapter is allowed.
- **Two safeguards.** On the drill script its first rewrites invented
  "facts" (casing reinforced every few hundred metres, bits lasting
  hours). So it is told never to invent a fact: if unsure, it uses an
  everyday example or a consequence of something the film has already
  said. It also runs before the fact check, so its lines are checked
  too.

**Brighter days.** Why, Though?'s look said "against a deep navy or
indigo background", which made every picture night-like. It now keeps
navy for space, night and underground, and asks for bright daylight
colours (sky blue, warm sand, fresh green, white cloud) for anything in
daylight. The geometric style is unchanged.

**Real chapter titles** (`outline.clean`, `problems`).
- **The bug.** A chapter the model called only "Cold open" was named
  after its turn. The turn was once a production note, so the video's
  first chapter would have read "The question is stated as a promise:
  if rivers are already".
- **The fix.** An untitled chapter now counts as a problem, so the
  outline is written again. If it's still untitled, the chapter takes
  the first sentence of what it says.

**The fact check doubts the film's own thesis.** The salt film's outline
argued that "salt never leaves the sea". That's false: it leaves slowly,
buried in seafloor mud and taken up at undersea vents, which is why the
sea stopped getting saltier long ago. The fact check took the thesis on
trust. It's now told to check the central claims too, and to treat an
absolute that is only mostly true ("never", "always", "no way out") as
a fault. Run again with that instruction, it corrected the claim in four
places, including "It's salty because it loses salt far slower than it
arrives". Two lines it missed were fixed by hand.

**A copied marker.** The repeat pass shows lines to the model as
"[chapter 3] ...", and the model once copied that into the narration.
It's stripped now; the fact check had also caught it.

**Nothing for the drawer to borrow.** The salt film's pictures had
glasses of water on cliffs, salt flats and hilltops, and an old man and
a child nobody asked for. There were two sources, and both are fixed for
documentaries; the shorts' animation is unchanged:
- **The style sheet.** Every still is drawn with it as its first
  reference, and it was a grid of studies that included an old man, a
  laughing child and a cup. That is very likely also where the first
  drill film's recurring bearded man and boy came from. Films now use a
  sheet of studies with nothing in it to copy (`bible.FILM_STYLE_SHEET`:
  a lit sphere, a stone cube, hills, cloud, water, rock, a leaf).
- **The "colour script".** The outline's visual concept was sent with
  every picture, and it said to show "the same two plain glasses ...
  wherever the scene allows them (a windowsill, a cliff-edge table...)".
  The drawer no longer gets it. The writer places a motif where it
  belongs, and every direction already names its light.

**Cards.**
- **Number cards fit their final value.** A number card held "0"
  while it was sized, so "100,000,000 years" ran off it. It now holds
  its final value from the start; the count-up overwrites it.
- **List cards are corrected with their line.** The fact check could
  only correct cards with "label|value" items. A list card kept saying
  salt has "nowhere to drain" after its line was corrected.
